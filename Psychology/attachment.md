# Institutionalisation: The effects of living in an institutional setting (e.g. orphanages), especially during early attachment.
- A critic of Bowlby's maternal deprivation theory is Micheal Rutter. Although he broadly agreed with Bowlby that early relationships
  were crucial, but he believed his theory lacked scientific precision.

- Deprivation: The disruption or loss of an already formed attachment bond between a child and their primary caregiver.
- Privation: The absence of any attachment figure during a child's early years. The child never forms an attachment bond with a primary
  caregiver.

## Effects:
- Disinhibited attachment: When children show a lack of caution around stranger and indiscriminate friendliness.
- Cognitive impairment: Problems with memory, language, thinking or judgement
- Delayed physical development: When a child takes longer to reach milestones such as sitting, crawling or walking
- Emotional problems: Problems with understanding and regulating emotions
- Disorganised attachment: An insecure attachment type, characterised by push and pull dynamics

## Emily C Merz (2013) The Russian Orphan Study
Objective - The study compared parent-rated excecutive functionig 
in 6-18 year old children adopted from psychologically depriving
(global) institutions to children adopted from severely depriving
institutions.

Method - There were 471 children adopted from psychologically 
depriving Russian institutions which provided adequate physical
resources but failed to proide a consistent set of responsive 
caregivers. There were 111 children adopted in the early 1990s
from globally depriving Romanian insitutions which were 
characterised by both physical deprivation and psychological 
neglect. Adoptive parents completed a background questionare
and the Behaviour Rating Inventory of Executive Function (BRIEF).

Results - Children adopted from psychologically depriving 
institutions had significantly higher level of executive function 
difficulties. For both groups, adoption after 18 months was
associated with higher levels of EF diffiulties.
Chilcren adopted from globally depriving institutions had higher 
levels of EF difficulties at younger ages at adoptions.
There was moderate to strong continuity in EF difficulties over 
2 years in the children adopted from psychologically depriving
institutions.

Conclusion - More severe early deprivation may lead to higher risk
of later EF difficulties.

- Critical period: The first 2-3 years are crucial for forming secure attachments
- Sensitive period: Up to 5 years of age - child is still sensitive to loss

- Consequences of deprivation: 
  - Long-term emotional and developmental difficulties, such as affectionless psychopathy, learning difficulties, and problems forming
    healthy relationships.

Bowlby argued that if a child experiences seperation or deprivation before the age of 2-3 years, it may have serious effects on their
emotional development.
